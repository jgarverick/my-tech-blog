Not every launch goes the way you planned it, and anyone who has spent time in tech knows that better than most. The Kickstarter campaign for Clown Migration Plan ended on April 2 with $2,793 pledged from 29 backers, well short of its $30,000 goal. Kickstarter campaigns are all or nothing, so no one was charged for their pledge.

First and foremost, thank you to everyone who backed the campaign, along with anyone who stopped by to take a look. As I said in my last campaign update, 'tis better to have launched and lost than never to have launched at all.

## What went well

Even without reaching the goal, the campaign period produced a few milestones worth calling out:

- **An in-person preview:** In late January, I was fortunate enough to bring a special edition of the game to my colleagues at Xebia for an in-person event. That was the last printing to use the original layout and font formatting.
- **An online playtesting site:** In early March, I put up a beta version of a playtesting site, which lets folks see how a game plays out, how the Purchase Pool gets calculated, and how some of the Backlog and card mechanics work, all without needing a physical copy in hand.
- **New prototype components:** A week later, the newest box, board, and card layouts arrived, including accordion-fold boards for the player areas and a bifold board for the Event Stream.
- **A community game night:** In mid-March, I dropped in on a local board gaming group in Batavia, New York, where four brave souls joined me for a five-player cooperative game. It was the first playtest with the new prototype boards and updated cards.

## What playtesting taught me

The Batavia game ran about an hour and 45 minutes, mostly because of explaining setup and the round mechanics as we went, and the group gave me some fantastic feedback on what they liked, what they didn't, and what might be missing. The biggest takeaway was that even in co-op, the game may be too difficult. Few cards allow for Influence increases, and there aren't enough promotable cards to support a good cooperative experience.

The group also had some great suggestions that I'm taking seriously:

- Separate decks for competitive and cooperative play, similar to the DC deckbuilders
- Keywords or iconography that call out each card's primary action, such as moving a Feature to Done
- Influence multipliers for each promotion, so a second promotion is worth more than the first

## What I learned from the campaign

Running a crowdfunding campaign is its own discipline, separate from designing the game itself, and it's important to note that a solid game doesn't automatically translate into a successful campaign.

## What comes next

Clown Migration Plan isn't going anywhere, and this won't be the last time it goes out to the general population. In the meantime, keep an eye out for a special edition run of the No-Frills version of the game on The Game Crafter. All of the assets are already uploaded, so it's just a matter of sorting out packaging and listing it on the marketplace.

Again, thank you to everyone who supported the campaign. Be sure to follow the project on [Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/jgarverick/clown-migration-plan-a-co-no-op-deckbuilder) so you hear about the next launch first, which I'll make sure is a successful one. Stay tuned!
